
1. Overview
   As a senior Electrical and Computer Engineering (ECE) student, you are expected to design systems that are not only technically sound, but also ethically responsible and socially aware. Engineering decisions can directly affect public safety, privacy, the environment, and societal trust.

This assignment requires you to critically analyze the ethical, professional, and societal implications of your senior design project. You will evaluate realistic engineering dilemmas, apply professional ethical standards, and assess the broader impact of your engineering work.

2. Learning Objectives
   This assignment is aligned with ABET Student Outcome 4:An ability to recognize ethical and professional responsibilities in engineering situations and make informed judgments, which must consider the impact of engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts.

SO4.1: Ethical and Professional Responsibility
Identify ethical and professional issues in engineering practice.
Apply professional codes of ethics to engineering situations.
Make informed and defensible ethical decisions.
Demonstrate honesty, accountability, and professional integrity.
SO4.2: Impact of Engineering Solutions
Evaluate engineering solutions in global, economic, environmental, and societal contexts.
Consider safety, sustainability, equity, accessibility, and public welfare.
Recognize the broader consequences of engineering decisions.
3. Assignment Instructions
Prepare an individual report analyzing the ethical and professional aspects of your own senior design project. Your report must include the following sections.

3.1 Project Context and System Overview
Provide a clear and concise description of your project.

System purpose and functionality.
Intended users and operating environment.
Key components, technologies, or methods used.
Potential misuse, unintended use, or limitations.
3.2 Stakeholder Identification and Interests
Identify all relevant stakeholders affected by your project.

Primary stakeholders, such as direct users.
Secondary stakeholders, such as operators, clients, regulators, or maintainers.
Indirect stakeholders, such as the public, environment, or vulnerable groups.
Stakeholder interests, risks, benefits, and possible harms.
3.3 Ethical Issue Identification
Identify major ethical and professional issues related to your project.

Identify 2 distinct ethical issues.
Cover multiple categories where applicable, such as safety, privacy, fairness, environmental impact, regulatory compliance, cybersecurity, intellectual property, or truthful reporting.
For each issue, include:
A brief description of the issue.
Relevant Saudi Council of Engineers (SCE) Engineer Charter code. [www.saudieng.sa/Admin/Documents/EngineerCharter2025-Ar.pdf](https://www.saudieng.sa/Admin/Documents/EngineerCharter2025-Ar.pdf)
3.4 Ethical Analysis and Decision-Making
Analyze each ethical issue and justify your decisions.

Clearly define the dilemma.
Identify at least two possible courses of action.
Evaluate alternatives using:
Ethical principles.
Professional responsibilities.
Stakeholder impacts.
Relevant Saudi Council of Engineers (SCE) Engineer Charter code. [www.saudieng.sa/Admin/Documents/EngineerCharter2025-Ar.pdf](https://www.saudieng.sa/Admin/Documents/EngineerCharter2025-Ar.pdf)
Select and justify the most ethical course of action.
3.5 Safety, Risk, and Compliance Analysis
Evaluate safety risks and regulatory responsibilities.

Key hazards, such as electrical, mechanical, software, AI, biomedical, environmental, or operational risks.
Qualitative risk assessment, including severity and likelihood.
Applicable standards, regulations, approvals, or institutional policies.
Proposed mitigation measures, including technical and procedural controls.
3.6 Data, Privacy, and Security Considerations
Analyze how your system handles data.

Data collection, processing, storage, sharing, and retention.
Consent, notification, and transparency.
Confidentiality, anonymization, and access control.
Cybersecurity risks and safeguards.
Potential misuse or unauthorized access.
If your project does not collect or process data, briefly explain why this section is not applicable.

3.7 Broader Impact Analysis
Evaluate the wider implications of your project using the following four contexts.

Global: standards, scalability, international relevance, and responsible innovation.
Economic: cost, affordability, trade-offs, liability, and long-term value.
Environmental: energy use, materials, sustainability, waste, and life-cycle impact.
Societal: privacy, equity, accessibility, public trust, inclusion, and quality of life.
3.8 Professional Integrity and Communication
Assess how your project should be represented and communicated.

Risks of misleading claims, exaggerated performance, or selective reporting.
Importance of accurate and transparent communication.
Acknowledgment of system limitations and uncertainty.
Accountability for errors, failures, or misuse.
3.9 Intellectual Property and Attribution
Discuss ethical use of external resources.

Use of external code, circuits, designs, datasets, algorithms, models, papers, or documentation.
Proper citation and acknowledgment.
Licensing considerations, including open-source and proprietary materials.
Ethical boundaries for reuse, modification, and distribution.
3.10 Recommendations and Mitigation Plan
Propose practical actions to improve ethical and professional quality.

Preventive actions before testing, demonstration, deployment, or publication.
Corrective actions in case of failure, harm, error, or misuse.
Technical, procedural, or policy-based recommendations.
Prioritization of the most important actions.
3.11 Reflection and Professional Responsibility
Reflect on your role as a future engineer.

The most significant ethical challenge in your project.
Key trade-offs encountered during design.
Your responsibility as a professional engineer.
How you would act in a real-world engineering scenario.
