# Saudi Council of Engineers — Engineer Charter 2025

Working project reference based on the uploaded Arabic **Engineer Charter 2025** issued by the Saudi Council of Engineers (SCE).

> This Markdown file is a project-use index/summary. The original Arabic PDF remains the authoritative source.

## Source information

- Title: `ميثاق المهندس` — Engineer Charter
- Issuer: Saudi Council of Engineers (SCE)
- First edition: 2025
- Issued based on Board Decision No. (25/8/10), dated 26 Jan 2025.

## Main charter objectives relevant to this project

The charter emphasizes:

1. professional competence, merit, honesty and fair competition;
2. continuous professional development;
3. commitment to professional ethics;
4. avoiding conflicts of interest and providing services honestly and objectively;
5. professional safety standards and protection of the public interest.

## Project-relevant rules

### Rule 1 — Honesty, integrity and public interest

Engineering professionals should act with honesty, integrity and moderation and avoid conduct that harms the public interest for personal benefit.

### Rule 6 — Intellectual property

Respect the intellectual-property rights of others and do not infringe or damage those rights.

**Project use:** external code, algorithms, datasets, papers, network models and documentation should be cited and used under appropriate licenses/permissions.

### Rule 8 — Responsibility for engineering work and safety

Engineers should use their experience and skills for the benefit of the profession and the organizations they serve and should take responsibility for the safety of the engineering works and solutions they design and implement.

**Project use:** the BESS-planning software must not present unsafe/infeasible recommendations as valid engineering decisions.

### Rule 9 — Confidentiality

Protect the confidentiality of information, data, contracts and engineering work and do not publish them without appropriate authorization.

**Project use:** relevant if non-public grid, customer, load or operational datasets are used.

### Rule 11 — Conflict of interest

Avoid conflicts of interest and disclose any conflict that may arise in professional services or work.

### Rule 12 — Public interest, cost estimation and competence

When studying engineering contracts/services, consider the public interest in cost estimates and ensure studies/practice are based on appropriate competence and professional experience.

**Project use:** economic optimization should not hide reliability or safety trade-offs merely to obtain a lower cost.

### Rule 14 — Accuracy, objectivity, independence and evidence

Professional decisions, consultations and technical reports should be accurate, objective and independent. Reports should be clear and supported by evidence that verifies the information and recommendations, while maintaining honesty and professional integrity.

**Project use:** report uncertainty, assumptions, validation results, failed requirements and limitations honestly. Do not overstate optimizer performance.

### Rule 15 — Public interest and safety standards

Promote the public interest, follow safety standards/measures, provide professional services consistently with those standards, and notify relevant parties when safety standards are violated.

**Project use:** infeasible voltage, SOC, network or safety conditions should be treated as constraints/failures rather than accepted because they improve the objective value.

### Rule 16 — Quality, sustainability and reliability

Engineers should ensure their professional services have high quality and value and contribute to sustainability and reliability.

**Project use:** directly relevant to renewable integration, BESS planning, reliability, curtailment reduction and responsible decision support.

## Recommended ethical issues for this project

The individual ethical-analysis assignment requires **two distinct issues**. Strong project-specific options are:

1. **Truthful reporting under model uncertainty** — risk of presenting a BESS recommendation as reliable when it depends on uncertain forecasts, scenario selection, assumptions or optimizer behavior. Relevant rules: 1, 14 and 16.
2. **Economic optimization versus grid reliability/public safety** — risk of choosing a cheaper configuration that performs poorly under voltage, load-shedding, SOC or reliability conditions. Relevant rules: 8, 12, 15 and 16.

Other applicable topics include confidentiality of datasets (Rule 9) and intellectual-property/attribution issues for code, datasets and algorithms (Rule 6).
